# KNF Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) and Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ)
**How to ferment plant and fruit material into potent foliar feeds for vegetative and reproductive growth stages.**
*For growers making their own organic nitrogen and plant hormone inputs.*

---

Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) and Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ) are the primary liquid nutritional inputs in Korean Natural Farming. FPJ is made from rapidly growing plant material and used during the vegetative stage. FFJ is made from ripe or overripe fruit and used during the reproductive/fruiting stage.

Both inputs are made using the same brown sugar fermentation method. The ratio is always 1 part plant/fruit material to 1 part brown sugar by weight. Cane molasses (jaggery) can substitute for brown sugar.

## Choosing Your Plant Material

### For FPJ (Vegetative-Stage Input)

Use plant material at its peak growth energy — the fastest-growing tip of the plant. The energy must be captured at the right moment:

- **Best sources:** Bamboo shoots (rapidly growing tip), watercress, arrowroot, comfrey, mugwort, dandelion tips, sweet potato vine tips, or the growing tips of any vigorous weed in your area.
- **For peppers specifically:** Comfrey is ideal — it is deep-rooted, concentrates calcium and potassium, and grows back rapidly after cutting. Moringa (Moringa oleifera) is excellent in Florida — fast-growing, high in cytokinins, and heat-tolerant.
- **Harvest timing:** Collect early morning before full sun, when the plant is full of moisture and energy.
- **Do not wash the material** — you want the natural microbes and yeasts on the surface.

> **Matt Powers (Permaculture Research Institute):** "The secret of FPJ is timing and selection. You're capturing the plant at peak biological activity — growth hormones, amino acids, enzymes. Wash it and you lose much of what you're after."

### For FFJ (Fruiting-Stage Input)

Use ripe or overripe fruit when sugars are highest. Common choices:
- Banana, papaya, mango (tropical preferred in Florida)
- Overripe tomatoes, peppers, or melons from the garden
- Any fruit reaching peak sweetness

Harvest just before the fruit would begin to rot.

## How to Make FPJ / FFJ

**Materials:**
- Plant or fruit material
- Raw granulated brown sugar (equal weight)
- Clean glass jar (wide mouth preferred)
- Muslin cloth or paper towels
- Rubber bands
- Weight (smaller jar of water, or flat stone)

**Process:**
1. Loosely chop plant material. Do not wash.
2. Weigh the plant material.
3. Mix with an equal weight of brown sugar. Work in the sugar until all material is coated.
4. Pack tightly into a clean glass jar until 2/3 full.
5. Place a weight on top to keep material submerged.
6. Cover with breathable cloth secured by rubber bands — never plastic, which blocks airflow.
7. Store at room temperature away from direct sunlight.
8. After 24 hours, liquid will begin to pool at the bottom. Stir gently with a wooden stick.
9. After 3–5 days, the plant material will have partially dissolved into a rich dark liquid. Bubbling is normal.
10. Strain out solids through cloth into a clean bottle. The liquid is your FPJ/FFJ concentrate.
11. Solids can be composted or added to IMO3.

**Storage:** Store in a cool, dark location. If storing long-term, add a small amount of brown sugar or alcohol (like soju/vodka) as a preservative. Stir before use.

## How to Use FPJ and FFJ

**Standard dilution: 1:500 to 1:1,000**

- For foliar sprays: 1:500 (1 part FPJ to 500 parts water) to 1:1,000
- For soil drench: 1:500 to 1:800

**As part of the standard maintenance cocktail:**
Mix FPJ + OHN + BRV in water at 1:500–1:800 total dilution, applied every 7–10 days as a foliar spray. This is the backbone of KNF maintenance.

**FPJ Timeline:**
- Vegetative stage: apply FPJ weekly or every 10 days
- Transition to fruiting: discontinue FPJ, switch to FFJ + WCA

**FFJ Timeline:**
- Once flower buds form: switch to FFJ at 1:500–1:1,000
- Combine with WCA and OHN during reproductive stage

## Florida-Specific Material Notes

In Zone 10a/10b Florida:
- **Moringa oleifera** is one of the best FPJ sources — grows explosively in Florida heat, contains high cytokinins (plant growth hormones), and is nearly year-round.
- **Sweet potato vines** grow wild in Florida and make excellent FPJ.
- **Comfrey** (Russian comfrey, Symphytum × uplandicum) can be established and cut frequently.
- **Papaya** and **banana** make outstanding FFJ and are locally available.
- Avoid using material sprayed with pesticides, herbicides, or chemical fertilizers.
