# KNF Seawater, Fermented Seawater, and Brown Rice Vinegar (BRV)
**Mineral-rich inputs for ripening, sugar development, and fungal disease control.**
*For growers improving fruit sweetness and preventing late-season disease.*

---

Seawater, fermented seawater, and brown rice vinegar (BRV) are supporting inputs in Korean Natural Farming. They are particularly valuable in the final stages of crop production — boosting sugar content, preventing certain fungal diseases, and balancing soil pH. For IC SPICY pepper production in Florida, all three have practical applications.

---

## Seawater

### What It Does

Seawater is a complete trace mineral solution containing over 70 elements in bioavailable form. At the correct dilution, it supports ripening and increases the sugar content (brix) of fruit. It can also treat fungal diseases like anthracnose.

**Critical warning:** Undiluted or improperly diluted seawater will burn plants. Always dilute correctly.

### How to Use Seawater

**Standard dilution: 1:30 (seawater to fresh water)**

For sugar content / ripening boost:
- Mix 1 part clean ocean seawater with 30 parts fresh water
- Apply as a foliar spray or soil drench during the final ripening stage
- As fruit approaches maturity, increase concentration slightly: 1:25 or 1:20

**For fruit ripening progression (CGNF protocol):**
- Early ripening: 1:30 dilution
- Mid ripening: 1:25 dilution
- Late ripening/color: 1:20 dilution

**Other uses:**
- Seedling treatment for onions, garlic, big leeks: diluted seawater soak before transplant
- Prevention of feather loss in chickens during hot summers

**Sourcing in Florida:** Clean seawater away from urban runoff or ports. The Florida Gulf coast (away from industrial areas) and Atlantic coast both work. Test salinity if possible — ocean standard is approximately 35 ppt. Avoid water during algae blooms.

## Fermented Seawater

### What It Does

Fermented seawater combines seawater's minerals with beneficial microbes from FPJ. It is more powerful than plain seawater for disease control, particularly against anthracnose (a major fungal threat to pepper crops).

### How to Make Fermented Seawater

**Recipe (from CGNF official materials):**
- 1 part seawater, diluted 1:30 with fresh water
- Plus rice-washed water at 1:200 dilution
- Plus FPJ (mugwort + Japanese dropwort blend) at 1:500 dilution
- Mix together and keep for half a day in summer (2–3 days if overcast or in cooler weather)

**Ratio summary (approximate by volume):**
- 1,000 mL freshwater base
- Add 33 mL seawater (1:30 ratio)
- Add 5 mL rice-washed water (1:200)
- Add 2 mL FPJ (1:500)
- Let sit in warm conditions for 12–72 hours

### How to Use Fermented Seawater

- Apply as a foliar spray during the fruiting and ripening stages
- Effective against **anthracnose** — a significant pepper disease in Florida's humid climate
- Apply preventively every 10–14 days during humid or rainy season
- Can be combined with WCA and OHN in a mineral-immune cocktail

## Brown Rice Vinegar (BRV)

### What It Does

Brown rice vinegar (BRV) is a naturally fermented vinegar made from whole brown rice. It is acidic (pH ~3) and serves multiple roles in KNF:

- **Extractant:** Used to dissolve calcium from eggshells (for WCA) and phosphate from bones (for WCP)
- **Spray adjunct:** Applied as part of the standard OHN + FPJ + BRV maintenance cocktail to deter powdery mildew and downy mildew
- **Soil pH balance:** Can help acidify overly alkaline soils when applied as a soil drench
- **Preservative:** Used in preparing OHN and storing fermented inputs

BRV is **not the same as white distilled vinegar or apple cider vinegar**. Brown rice vinegar is made through a longer fermentation process and retains a complex array of organic acids and trace nutrients. Substituting white vinegar will change the pH and chemistry.

### How to Use BRV

**For WCA / WCP extraction:** Use undiluted as the solvent (no dilution needed — it is the extractant).

**As foliar spray component:**
- Dilute 1:1,000 in water as part of the OHN + FPJ + BRV cocktail
- Apply every 10–12 days to prevent mildew
- Can apply weekly during particularly humid Florida summers

**As soil drench:**
- Dilute 1:1,000 to 1:500 and apply to overly alkaline soil
- Never over-apply — excess acid harms soil biology

**Sourcing BRV:** Available at Asian grocery stores, natural food stores, and online. Look for "brown rice vinegar" made through traditional fermentation — not synthetic acetic acid.

## Florida Notes

- **Anthracnose** (*Colletotrichum* spp.) is one of the most destructive diseases on Florida peppers during the humid summer. Fermented seawater + OHN cocktail is a natural prevention protocol.
- **Florida seawater** is accessible statewide. For Zone 10a growers near Miami, the Atlantic or Florida Bay are both viable sources.
- **Brix testing:** A refractometer (< $20) lets you measure sugar content of your juice and spray water. Target 8–12 brix in foliar spray water for best plant uptake. Adding seawater inputs improves brix in fruit over time.
