# KNF Water-Soluble Calcium (WCA) and Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WCP)
**How to make bioavailable calcium and phosphate from eggshells and bones — critical for fruiting crops.**
*For growers preventing blossom-end rot and supporting strong fruit development.*

---

Water-Soluble Calcium (WCA) and Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WCP) are KNF's mineral inputs for the reproductive stage. They address two of the most common failures in fruiting crops:

- **WCA** prevents calcium deficiency in rapidly expanding fruit tissue — the root cause of blossom-end rot in peppers, tomatoes, and squash.
- **WCP** provides phosphorus in plant-available form for flowering, fruit set, and seed development.

Both inputs are made at home from kitchen waste using brown rice vinegar (BRV) as the extractant.

---

## Water-Soluble Calcium (WCA)

### What It Does

Calcium is essential for cell wall integrity, root growth, and fruit quality. Plants cannot move calcium from leaves to fruit — it must be delivered directly to actively growing fruit tissue via transpiration flow. Blossom-end rot is not caused by a lack of calcium in the soil; it is caused by poor calcium delivery to rapidly expanding fruit tissue. WCA applied as a foliar spray delivers calcium directly to the fruit surface and leaf tissue.

WCA also supports seed germination: calcium-deficient seeds germinate poorly and produce weak seedlings.

### How to Make WCA

**Materials:**
- Eggshells (poultry), oyster shells, or clam shells (all work)
- Brown rice vinegar (BRV) — a weak acid (~3% acetic acid) that dissolves calcium carbonate
- Clean glass jar
- Muslin cloth or paper towel
- Rubber bands

**Process:**
1. Collect eggshells and remove the tough inner membrane.
2. Break shells into ¼-inch pieces.
3. Lightly roast shells in a frying pan or foil over low heat for approximately 45 minutes.
   - Goal: dry, lightly tan color — not charred or white-grey.
   - This removes organic substances that would rot in the vinegar.
4. Fill a clean glass jar 2/3 full with brown rice vinegar.
5. Slowly add roasted eggshells to the vinegar at a **1:10 ratio by weight** (shells to vinegar).
   - Fragments will float and emit carbon dioxide bubbles as calcium dissolves into solution.
6. Cover with breathable cloth and secure with rubber bands.
7. Store in a cool, dark location for **7–10 days**.
8. Check for bubbles after 7 days:
   - **No bubbles:** Solution is saturated — proceed to step 9.
   - **Still bubbling:** Add more roasted eggshells and wait 1–2 more days. Repeat until bubbling stops.
9. Strain the solution through a colander to remove shells. The strained liquid is your WCA.
10. Label and store at room temperature out of direct sunlight. No refrigeration required.

### How to Use WCA

**Standard dilution: 1:1,000**

| Water Volume | Kitchen Measure | mL |
|---|---|---|
| ½ gallon | ⅓ tsp | 2 |
| 1 gallon | ¾ tsp | 4 |
| 5 gallons | 1¼ Tbsp | 19 |
| 10 gallons | 2½ Tbsp | 38 |
| 25 gallons | just under ½ cup | 95 |

**Application:**
- Apply as a foliar spray during the **reproductive stage** (flowering through fruiting)
- Spray just after sundown or very early morning to prevent leaf burn
- Use a light mist — do not spray to the point of run-off
- Apply every 7–10 days once flower buds appear

**In seed soak:**
- Combine WCA with OHN and LAB for pre-plant seed treatment
- Improves germination, seedling vigor, and microbial inoculation

## Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WCP)

### What It Does

Calcium phosphate delivers phosphorus in a form plants can absorb. Phosphorus drives flowering, root development, fruit set, and seed maturation. WCP is the KNF input for transition from vegetative to reproductive growth.

### How to Make WCP

**Materials:**
- Bones of vertebrates: pig bones, cow bones, or fish bones — all contain calcium phosphate
- Fermented Brown Rice Vinegar (BRV) as the extractant
- Clay jar, cedar bucket, or glass jar
- Porous paper (Korean paper or paper towel)
- Rubber band

**Process:**
1. Source bones from a butcher or meat processor. Fish bones are especially high in phosphate.
2. Burn the bones at a low temperature — enough to remove organic matter, not to reduce them to ash. Bones should be chalky white/grey.
   - Low-temperature burning removes organic substances while leaving inorganic calcium phosphate intact.
3. Once cooled, add the burned bones to a jar of BRV.
   - The acid extracts the inorganic calcium phosphate from the bones.
4. Cover with porous paper. Allow to extract in a cool, dark location for 7–10 days.
5. Strain liquid. The liquid is WCP concentrate.

### How to Use WCP

- Apply at **1:1,000 dilution** as a foliar spray
- Use during the transition stage when flower buds first appear
- Combine with WCA in a mineral cocktail: WCA + WCP + OHN at 1:1,000 each in one spray
- Apply every 10–14 days during the reproductive phase

---

## Combined Mineral Spray for Fruiting

Standard pepper and tomato reproductive-stage cocktail (per gallon of water):
- ¾ tsp WCA
- ¾ tsp WCP
- ¾ tsp OHN
- Apply in early morning or at dusk, every 7–10 days

This cocktail reduces blossom-end rot, supports strong fruit set, and builds sugar content.
