# KNF Fish Amino Acid (FAA)
**How to ferment fish byproducts into a plant-available nitrogen source for the vegetative growth stage.**
*For growers wanting a biological nitrogen input that improves yields without synthetic fertilizer.*

---

Fish Amino Acid (FAA) is a fermented liquid made from fish waste — head, bones, skin, fins, and viscera — combined with brown sugar. The fermentation process breaks down proteins into free amino acids, creating a bioavailable nitrogen source that both plants and soil microorganisms use efficiently.

FAA is one of the most important KNF nitrogen inputs. It is applied during the vegetative growth stage to boost plant size and leaf development. **Do not apply FAA during flowering or fruiting — excess nitrogen at that stage delays ripening and reduces fruit quality.**

## Choosing Your Fish

Use deep-sea, blue-back fish for best results. These are high in omega fatty acids, protein, and minerals. Common choices:
- Mackerel
- Sardines
- Skipjack tuna
- Herring
- Menhaden (widely available as bycatch in Florida)

Source fish waste from local fish markets, docks, or seafood restaurants. Fresh is better than frozen.

## How to Make FAA

**Materials:**
- Fish waste (head, bones, skin, fins, viscera)
- Raw granulated brown sugar (equal weight to fish waste)
- Fermentation container (clay jar or food-grade plastic cooler)
- Large rocks (for aeration layer at bottom)
- A handful of IMO4 (optional, to accelerate fermentation)
- A small amount of OHN (optional, to activate IMO)
- Breathable cloth + rubber bands for cover

**Process:**
1. Collect fresh fish waste and weigh it.
2. Mix fish waste with an equal weight of brown sugar (1:1 by weight). Mix thoroughly.
3. Place a layer of large rocks at the bottom of the container — this allows liquid to pool below and provides aeration.
4. Add alternating layers: fish-sugar mixture, then more brown sugar, fish-sugar mixture, then more brown sugar. End with a layer of pure brown sugar. No fish should be exposed to air.
5. Optionally add a handful of IMO4 and a small pour of OHN to accelerate fermentation.
6. Cover with breathable cloth to keep out insects while allowing airflow.
7. Store out of direct sunlight in a cool, well-ventilated location. Secure from animals.
8. After 3–5 days, the fish will begin to liquefy through osmotic pressure and fermentation.
9. The process takes **2–6 months** to complete. Mature FAA has a sweet, slightly fishy odor.
10. Decant (pour off) only the **liquid portion** to use as FAA.
11. The remaining solids can be used to make IMO5 or added to compost.

## How to Use FAA

**Standard dilution: 1:1,000**

| Water Volume | Kitchen Measure | mL |
|---|---|---|
| ½ gallon | ⅓ tsp | 2 |
| 1 gallon | ¾ tsp | 4 |
| 5 gallons | 1¼ Tbsp | 19 |
| 10 gallons | 2½ Tbsp | 38 |
| 25 gallons | just under ½ cup | 95 |
| 50 gallons | just over ¾ cup | 189 |

**As foliar spray:**
- Apply as a light foliar mist at 1:1,000 dilution.
- Combine with FPJ, OHN, and/or LAB in a cocktail spray.
- Apply in the early morning or late afternoon — not in full midday sun (risk of foliar burn and rapid evaporation).
- For leafy vegetables: spray weekly to improve yields, fragrance, and taste.

**As soil drench:**
- Apply at 1:500 to 1:1,000 directly to the soil around plant roots.
- Maximizes uptake and minimizes runoff and leaching.

**Do NOT apply FAA:**
- During the flowering stage — nitrogen delays fruit set
- During the fruiting stage — nitrogen reduces fruit quality and sweetness
- Switch to WCA, WCP, and FFJ once flower buds appear

## Florida Notes for Peppers

FAA is especially valuable for Florida pepper growing:
- Apply every 7–10 days during the vigorous vegetative growth phase (first 4–6 weeks after transplant)
- Peppers in Zone 10a grow aggressively in the warm season — match FAA applications to plant energy
- Stop FAA completely at first blossom appearance
- Menhaden is Florida's most accessible blue-back fish waste and makes excellent FAA
