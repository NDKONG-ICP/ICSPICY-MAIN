# JADAM Sulfur (JS), Herbal Solution (JHS), and Wetting Agent (JWA)
**JADAM's three-input pest and disease control system — organic and effective against fungal and insect pressure.**
*For growers dealing with mold, mildew, and insect pests without synthetic pesticides.*

---

These three JADAM inputs work together as a complete organic pest and disease control system. JADAM Sulfur (JS) handles fungal diseases. JADAM Herbal Solution (JHS) handles insects and some pathogens. JADAM Wetting Agent (JWA) is added to both to dramatically improve penetration and coverage on plant surfaces.

All three are approved for USDA certified organic production. Youngsang Cho deliberately chose materials that meet organic standards.

---

## JADAM Sulfur (JS)

### What It Does

JADAM Sulfur is an organic fungicide made by dissolving elemental sulfur in a potassium hydroxide (KOH) solution. It is at least as effective as commercial lime-sulfur fungicide, but far less expensive. Unlike lime-sulfur, it does not damage steel pipes or plastic — a practical advantage for drip irrigation systems.

Invented by Youngsang Cho in 2011 and immediately made freely available to the public (no patent filed).

### How to Make JS

**Important:** This process requires handling potassium hydroxide (KOH / lye). KOH is caustic — wear gloves and eye protection. Work outdoors or in a well-ventilated space. Keep water nearby.

**Materials:**
- Elemental sulfur powder (garden sulfur, available at agricultural suppliers)
- Potassium hydroxide (KOH / food-grade lye) — available from homebrewing suppliers or online
- Soft water (rainwater strongly preferred)
- Stainless steel or heavy-duty plastic pot (do NOT use aluminum — KOH reacts with aluminum)
- Scale and measuring cups
- Protective gloves and eye protection

**Standard JS ratio (from JADAM official materials):**
- Elemental sulfur: 1 kg
- Potassium hydroxide (KOH): 1 kg
- Soft water: 5–6 liters

**Process:**
1. Measure and combine KOH and elemental sulfur in a large pot.
2. Add 5–6 liters of soft water gradually while stirring. The reaction generates heat — this is normal.
3. Stir continuously. The sulfur will dissolve into a dark brown/red solution. This is the chemical reaction — no external heat is needed.
4. Once the reaction is complete and the solution is fully dissolved (no visible sulfur particles), JS is ready.
5. Pour into clean, labeled containers. Keep tightly sealed and store away from sunlight.

**JADAM note:** The no-heat method was Youngsang Cho's invention. Traditional lime-sulfur requires boiling; JS does not. This makes it safer and simpler.

### How to Use JS

**Dilution: 100–500:1 (water to JS concentrate)**

- As a fungicide: dilute at 100:1 to 200:1 (1 tsp JS per 2 cups water = ~200:1)
- For severe infection: dilute at 50:1 to 100:1
- **Always add JWA to the JS spray** — wetting agent dramatically improves coverage

**Target diseases:**
- Powdery mildew (major pepper problem in Florida)
- Downy mildew
- Anthracnose (pepper fruit rot in humid conditions)
- Early and late blight
- Most foliar fungal infections

**Timing:** Apply in the morning or evening. Do not apply in full sun or at temperatures above 95°F — risk of phytotoxicity (leaf burn) increases with heat.

**Compatibility note:** Do NOT mix JS with copper-based sprays (Bordeaux mixture) in the same application — they react and reduce effectiveness of both.

## JADAM Wetting Agent (JWA)

### What It Does

JWA is a soap-based surfactant that reduces the surface tension of water, making spray droplets adhere to plant surfaces (including waxy pepper leaves) instead of beading off. Adding JWA to any spray — JS, JHS, or conventional — improves coverage and penetration by 3–5×.

### How to Make JWA

**Materials:**
- Plant-based oil (any: soybean, sunflower, canola, or rendered animal fat)
- Potassium hydroxide (KOH)
- Soft water
- Stainless or heavy plastic pot
- Protective gear

**Standard ratio:**
- Plant oil: 1 liter
- KOH: 200 grams
- Soft water: 500 mL

**Process:**
1. Combine KOH and soft water in a pot. Stir to dissolve. Mixture heats up — this is saponification.
2. Add plant oil gradually while stirring continuously.
3. Stir for 30–60 minutes until the mixture becomes a thick, opaque liquid soap (like a runny hand soap).
4. Let cool. Store in a sealed container.
5. Optionally dilute with additional soft water to desired consistency.

**Testing:** Pour a small amount of JWA into a glass of your spray water. It should form stable foam. If foam collapses immediately, your water is too hard — switch to rainwater.

### How to Use JWA

- Add 1–2 mL of JWA per liter of any spray solution (1:500 to 1:1,000 dilution)
- Always add JWA last, after mixing all other inputs
- Do not use JWA at high concentrations without testing — over-application can damage leaves

## JADAM Herbal Solution (JHS)

### What It Does

JHS is a hot-water extract of specific herbs that act as contact pesticides and pest deterrents. The three herbs most used in JADAM for JHS are:
- **Sunchoke** (Jerusalem artichoke, *Helianthus tuberosus*)
- **Ginkgo leaves** (*Ginkgo biloba*)
- **Dried roots of Korean pasque flower** (*Pulsatilla koreana*)

Different herbs target different pests. In North American contexts, locally available substitutes with similar pest-deterrent compounds may be identified.

### How to Make JHS

**Materials:**
- Fresh or dried herbs of choice (500g–1kg per batch)
- Soft water (10–15 liters)
- Large pot
- Cloth bag (to hold herbs — keeps the liquid clean)
- Clean bottles for storage

**Process:**
1. Place herbs in a cloth bag.
2. Place bag in a large pot with soft water.
3. Bring to a boil — takes approximately 1 hour.
4. Reduce heat after boiling to minimize evaporation loss.
5. Boil for **5 hours total** at a gentle simmer.
6. While still boiling, pour the liquid into clean bottles. Fill completely — leave **no air pocket**.
7. Immediately lay the filled bottle on its side. This creates a vacuum seal as it cools and allows long-term storage without refrigeration.

**Storage:** Bottles sealed this way keep for months without refrigeration.

### How to Use JHS

- Dilute at 100:1 to 200:1 with soft water
- Add JWA (1–2 mL per liter) to improve adhesion
- Apply as a thorough foliar spray, coating all leaf surfaces
- Reapply after rain or every 7–14 days during pest pressure
- Effective against: aphids, thrips, spider mites, and some caterpillar pests

## The JADAM Spray Stack for Florida Peppers

During Florida's rainy season (June–October), a combined preventive program:

**Every 7–10 days:**
- JS at 200:1 + JWA at 1:500 (fungicide/mildew prevention)

**When pest pressure observed:**
- JHS at 200:1 + JWA at 1:500

**Do not mix JS and JHS in the same spray** — apply on alternating days if both are needed simultaneously.
