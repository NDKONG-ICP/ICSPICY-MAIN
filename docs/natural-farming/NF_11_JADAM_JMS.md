# JADAM Microorganism Solution (JMS)
**How to make a powerful, simplified soil biology builder from leaf mold and potato water in a few days.**
*For growers who want KNF-style soil microbes without the multi-week IMO process.*

---

JADAM Microorganism Solution (JMS) is JADAM's answer to KNF's IMO — a much simpler, faster process to culture and apply beneficial soil microorganisms. It achieves similar results (activating soil biology, decomposing organic matter, building microbial diversity) in a fraction of the time.

JMS is also the base ingredient for JADAM Microbial Pesticide (JMP), where specific pathogens are grown in JMS and then deployed against their natural enemies.

## What Makes JMS Different from IMO

| Aspect | IMO (KNF) | JMS (JADAM) |
|---|---|---|
| Collection | Rice bait box buried under forest floor (3–5 days) | Leaf mold dug directly from forest floor |
| Culture medium | Brown sugar (hours of kneading) | Potato starch water (minutes to prepare) |
| Propagation | Wheat mill run compost pile (7 days, turning required) | Jar fermentation (2–5 days) |
| Total time | 3–4 weeks | 3–7 days |
| Complexity | High | Low |

## How to Make JMS

**Materials:**
- Leaf mold: a handful of well-decomposed organic material from under mature trees in an undisturbed area
- Potatoes: 3–4 medium potatoes
- Soft water: rainwater preferred; city water if necessary (do the foam test first)
- A large fermentation container (food-grade plastic tub, bucket, or jar — large enough for 20–30 liters)
- Breathable cloth cover

**Process:**

**Step 1 — Prepare potato water:**
1. Boil 3–4 medium-sized potatoes in soft water until the water becomes starchy and cloudy (about 20 minutes).
2. Alternatively, blend raw potatoes with water and strain. The starchy liquid is the growth medium.
3. Allow to cool to room temperature. Do not use hot liquid — it will kill the microbes.

**Step 2 — Collect leaf mold:**
1. Go to an undisturbed wooded area (forest edge, treeline, established garden border).
2. Dig 2–4 inches into the soil surface, below fresh leaves and into the partially decomposed leaf layer.
3. Collect 1–2 large handfuls of this dark, earthy-smelling material. This is your microbial inoculant.
4. Richer collection = more diverse microbes. Take from multiple spots if possible.

**Step 3 — Combine and culture:**
1. Add the leaf mold to the cooled potato water in your container.
2. Stir well.
3. Cover with breathable cloth.
4. Leave at room temperature (ideally 68–86°F / 20–30°C) for 2–5 days, stirring once daily.
5. The solution is ready when it smells earthy and pleasant (like healthy forest soil). A slight foam is normal and good. If it smells rotten or putrid, discard and try again with fresher leaf mold.

## How to Use JMS

**As soil drench:**
- Use undiluted or dilute 1:5 to 1:20 depending on how concentrated your culture is
- Apply generously to soil before planting and monthly as a maintenance drench
- Water into the root zone after application

**As spray:**
- Dilute 1:10 to 1:20 in soft water
- Apply as a light foliar mist in early morning or evening

**As a compost activator:**
- Pour undiluted JMS onto compost piles to accelerate decomposition

**For JMP (JADAM Microbial Pesticide):**
- Use JMS as the growth medium. Introduce a specific pathogen (e.g., a sick leaf with the target disease) and culture for 2–5 days.
- The resulting solution is sprayed on crops as a biological pest-specific control.
- This is an advanced application — consult Youngsang Cho's book on JADAM pest control before attempting.

## JMS in the Florida Context

In South Florida's warm climate:
- JMS cultures faster than in cooler climates — often ready in 2–3 days
- Collect leaf mold from under mature native Florida trees (live oak, gumbo limbo, strangler fig)
- Avoid collection near treated lawns, golf courses, or anywhere chemicals have been applied
- Rainwater is abundant in Florida — use it. Collect during summer rains for a year-round soft water supply.
