# KNF Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO)
**How to collect, culture, and apply locally-sourced soil microbes — the foundation of Korean Natural Farming.**
*For growers building soil biology from scratch or restoring degraded soil.*

---

Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO) are naturally occurring beneficial microbes collected from the local environment. In KNF, IMO is the single most important input. It builds the balanced microbial community that reduces pest pressure, decomposes organic matter, and makes nutrients available to plants.

IMO progresses through five stages: IMO1 (collection), IMO2 (liquid culture), IMO3 (field compost), IMO4 (finished amendment), IMO5 (optional, with fish byproducts). For most growers, IMO1 through IMO4 are the working cycle.

## IMO 1 — Collection

**Materials:**
- Wooden box approximately 12 × 12 × 4 inches, cedar preferred
- 3 inches of steamed white rice
- White paper towels (to cover without touching rice, leaving ~1 inch air gap)
- Wire mesh screen (1/4 inch) to keep animals out
- Clear plastic sheet for rain protection
- Rubber bands

**Process:**
1. Fill box with 3 inches of freshly steamed white rice.
2. Cover with paper towel secured by rubber bands — do not let paper touch rice.
3. Cover with wire mesh, then plastic sheet weighted with rocks.
4. Place under undisturbed forest or vegetated area, partially buried 2 inches into soil.
5. Cover with local fallen leaves.
6. Leave undisturbed 3–4 days (warm conditions above 68°F / 20°C) or 4–5 days (cooler).
7. Check for white mold covering the rice surface. If mold is sparse, wait 2–3 more days.
8. If mold is any color other than white, or if rain entered the box, discard and restart.

**Note from UH-CTAHR:** Combine microbes collected from multiple sites for a more diverse and robust culture. Avoid collection during wet or rainy seasons — excessive moisture promotes less desirable fungi.

## IMO 2 — Sugar Preservation

**Materials:**
- Molded rice from IMO1
- Raw granulated brown sugar (equal weight to rice mass)
- Clean clay pot

**Process:**
1. Transfer molded rice to a large bowl and weigh it.
2. Add an equal weight of brown sugar.
3. Hand-knead until the consistency resembles gooey molasses. Wear gloves.
4. Fill a clean clay pot two-thirds full with this mixture.
5. Cover with paper towel, secure with rubber bands.
6. Store in a cool area away from direct sunlight for 7 days to ferment.

This produces IMO2, a preserved culture ready for the next stage.

## IMO 3 — Field Compost Inoculation

**Materials:**
- IMO2 culture
- Water (ratio 1:500, IMO2 to water)
- Wheat mill run (or mushroom growth medium waste)

**Process:**
1. In a shaded area, mix IMO2 into water at a 1:500 ratio.
2. Blend in wheat mill run until the mixture has 65–70% moisture (semi-moist, not wet).
3. Place as a mound on a soil surface, cover with straw mat or leaves to protect from sun.
4. Allow microbes to propagate for 7 days.
5. Turn the pile 3–4 times during the week with a shovel to prevent overheating.
6. Monitor internal temperature with a composting thermometer — do not exceed 122°F (50°C).
7. Fermentation is complete when internal temperature stabilizes.

## IMO 4 — Final Amendment

IMO4 is IMO3 mixed further with soil amendments, compost, organic matter, and additional KNF inputs (OHN, FAA). It is the finished soil amendment applied before planting or as a top dressing.

Application rate from CGNF: approximately 1,200 lb per acre as soil incorporation.

For small-scale gardens: dilute final product 1:1 by volume with soil and incorporate as a top dressing.

## Application

- **Soil drench:** Dilute IMO2 or liquid IMO at 1:500 to 1:1,000 in water and drench the soil.
- **Foliar spray:** Dilute liquid IMO at 1:500 for foliar application.
- Standard application rates (Natural Farming Hawaii): 100 gallons/acre drench, 25 gallons/acre spray.

## Safety Notes

When handling fungal cultures, UH-CTAHR recommends:
- Work outdoors or in well-ventilated areas, never in enclosed rooms.
- Wear disposable gloves and an N95 respirator mask.
- Wash hands thoroughly before and after handling.
- Do not handle if you have open wounds, respiratory conditions, or compromised immunity.
