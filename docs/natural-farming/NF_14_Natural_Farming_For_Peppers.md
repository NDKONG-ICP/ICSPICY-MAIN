# Natural Farming for Peppers: The Complete Protocol
**Applying KNF and JADAM methods specifically to Capsicum production — from seed to harvest.**
*For IC SPICY growers running a complete natural farming program on superhot and rare pepper varieties.*

---

This document integrates Korean Natural Farming (KNF) and JADAM Organic Farming practices specifically for *Capsicum* production. Peppers have specific needs — calcium sensitivity, strong nitrogen response during vegetative growth, and particular disease pressures — that shape how natural farming inputs are deployed.

## Before You Start: The Soil Health Foundation

Every successful natural farming program starts with soil biology. Without a functioning microbial community in the soil, KNF inputs have little to work with — they feed microbes, which then feed plants. Soil that has been treated with synthetic fungicides, fumigants, or heavy doses of chemical fertilizers may need 2–4 seasons of biological recovery before KNF reaches its full potential.

**Assessment before planting:**
1. Smell your soil. Healthy, biologically active soil smells earthy, like a forest floor. Dead or chemically treated soil smells flat, or may smell of chemicals.
2. Look for earthworms. Healthy soil in Florida should support earthworm populations. No earthworms is a warning sign.
3. Dig and observe: crumbly, aggregated soil with visible fungal threads (thin white filaments) is excellent. Dense, compacted soil needs amendment.
4. For established beds: do a jar settle test (1 cup soil + 1 cup water, shake, let settle 24 hours) to observe texture ratios.

## Complete Pepper Farming Program

### Phase 1 — Soil Preparation (2 weeks before transplant)

**Week -2:**
- Amend beds with IMO4 (1 cup per plant site) and aged compost
- Soil drench: IMO liquid at 1:500 + LAB at 1:1,000
- If soil is very depleted: apply JMS drench at 1:10 dilution
- Mulch with straw, wood chips, or dried grass — this feeds the microbial community and retains moisture

**Week -1:**
- Soil drench: OHN at 1:1,000 to activate soil microorganisms
- Do not disturb soil after application

### Phase 2 — Transplant Day

**Seed soak (night before transplant or seeding):**
- In 1 quart of water: mix ¾ tsp WCA + ¾ tsp OHN + ¾ tsp LAB
- Soak seeds 8–12 hours OR dip transplant roots in solution for 10 minutes before planting

**At transplant:**
- Water in with IMO drench at 1:500
- Do not foliar spray for 3–5 days — let the plant establish root contact

### Phase 3 — Vegetative Growth (First 4–6 Weeks)

**Goals:** Build leaf mass and root system. Prioritize nitrogen and growth hormones.

**Every 7 days — foliar spray:**
- Per gallon: ¾ tsp FPJ + ¾ tsp OHN + ¾ tsp BRV
- Apply at dawn or dusk

**Every 14 days — nitrogen boost:**
- Per gallon: ¾ tsp FAA
- Apply as foliar mist or soil drench
- Alternate with the standard FPJ cocktail (don't apply same day)

**Every 30 days — soil biology maintenance:**
- Per gallon: ¾ tsp IMO + ¾ tsp LAB
- Drench around root zone

**Disease prevention (humid weather or rainy season):**
- Add OHN + BRV to weekly spray (already in standard cocktail)
- Begin JADAM JS + JWA spray every 14 days if mildew pressure observed

**Signs of healthy vegetative growth:**
- Leaves expanding rapidly, deep green color
- Thick main stem forming
- Roots visible at drain holes in containers (signal to size up or plant out)

### Phase 4 — Transition to Flowering

**Trigger:** First flower buds visible (small pale green bumps at branch axils).

**Immediately stop:**
- FAA (nitrogen delays flower set)
- FPJ (or significantly reduce)

**Begin:**
- WCA foliar spray at 1:1,000 every 7–10 days
- WCP foliar spray at 1:1,000 (combine with WCA)
- FFJ as a replacement for FPJ at 1:500–1:1,000

**Transition cocktail per gallon:**
- ¾ tsp WCA + ¾ tsp OHN + ¾ tsp FFJ
- Spray every 7 days

This is the critical pivot. Many growers are late to switch and end up with excessive leaf growth and delayed fruiting. Watch for first buds and respond immediately.

### Phase 5 — Flowering and Fruit Set

**Goals:** High flower count, strong fruit set, no blossom drop, no blossom-end rot.

**Every 7–10 days — calcium mineral spray:**
- Per gallon: ¾ tsp WCA + ¾ tsp WCP + ¾ tsp OHN
- Evening application only — protect delicate flowers from heat stress

**Blossom-end rot prevention protocol:**
- Consistent watering is as important as WCA — calcium moves via water flow. Irregular irrigation is the most common cause of BER even when WCA is being applied.
- Check soil moisture daily. Drip irrigation on a timer is ideal.
- Apply WCA every 7 days without fail once fruit set begins.

**Every 14 days — immune support:**
- Per gallon: ¾ tsp OHN + ¾ tsp BRV
- This cocktail has documented effectiveness against powdery and downy mildew (CTAHR SA-11)

### Phase 6 — Fruit Development and Ripening

**Goals:** Build brix (sugar), intensify capsaicin, develop color, prevent late-season fungal damage.

**Every 7–10 days — ripening spray:**
- Seawater diluted 1:30 + ¾ tsp OHN per gallon
- Increase seawater concentration as fruit colors: 1:25 then 1:20

**JADAM disease prevention (critical during Florida's wet season):**
- JS at 200:1 + JWA at 1:500 — every 7–14 days during anthracnose risk period
- Apply in morning, not in full midday sun

**Harvest timing for maximum capsaicin:**
- Most superhot varieties (C. chinense) peak in capsaicin after full color change
- The Scoville heat of a ripe Carolina Reaper is significantly higher than an unripe one
- Let fruit fully color before picking

## Quick Troubleshooting Guide

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Natural Farming Response |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow lower leaves | Nitrogen deficiency | FAA soil drench at 1:1,000 (vegetative stage only) |
| Blossom drop | Temperature stress or calcium | WCA foliar, consistent watering, shade cloth if >95°F |
| Blossom-end rot | Calcium delivery failure | WCA every 7 days, consistent irrigation |
| Sunken dark spots on fruit | Anthracnose | JS + JWA spray, remove infected fruit |
| White powder on leaves | Powdery mildew | OHN + BRV cocktail; JS + JWA for active infection |
| Distorted new growth | Thrips or broad mites | JHS + JWA spray; inspect under leaves with hand lens |
| Wilting despite moisture | Root rot (Phytophthora) | Improve drainage, LAB drench, reduce watering |
| No fruit set | Temperature (too hot or cold) | Wait; shade cloth above 95°F daytime |
| Small fruits | Phosphorus or potassium | WCP spray; FFJ during reproductive stage |

## Notes on Florida Superhot Production Specifically

Chris Trump, who trained under Master Cho for 12 years, emphasizes: "Natural farming is not about recipes — it's about building a system that self-corrects. Once your soil biology is healthy, your pest and disease problems drop by 70–80%. Most growers who fail at KNF are still fighting symptoms instead of building the foundation."

For superhot varieties in Zone 10a:
- Heat does not reduce capsaicin — Florida's climate is ideal for maximum heat development
- Irregular irrigation causes more problems than any other single factor
- The perennial nature of C. chinense in Zone 10a allows cumulative soil biology improvement season over season
