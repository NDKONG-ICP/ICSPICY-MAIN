# KNF Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB)
**How to culture LAB from rice rinse water and use it to protect crops, improve soil, and support plant health.**
*For growers wanting a free probiotic input from kitchen materials.*

---

Lactic acid bacteria (LAB) are naturally occurring beneficial microorganisms. In KNF they act as the "police" of the soil ecosystem, suppressing pathogenic microbes and supporting decomposition. LAB are the same organisms responsible for yogurt, kimchi, sauerkraut, and other fermented foods — they are safe for humans, animals, and plants.

LAB are easy to make, cost almost nothing, and store well under refrigeration for up to 6 months. The recipe below is the official Cho Global Natural Farming protocol, peer-reviewed and published by UH-CTAHR.

## How to Make LAB (Official CGNF / CTAHR Protocol)

**Materials:**
- Uncooked white rice
- Clean water
- Clean glass jar(s)
- Fresh cow's or goat's milk, preferably unpasteurized and antibiotic-free
- Muslin cloth or paper towels (breathable — do not use plastic)
- Rubber bands
- Brown sugar (for storage preservation)

**Step 1 — Collect Rice Rinse Water**
1. Wash rice grains with clean water. Collect the first two rinses of cloudy water.
2. Fill a clean glass jar about 2/3 full with rice rinse water.
3. Label the jar with contents and date.
4. Cover with breathable cloth secured by rubber bands. Do not use plastic.
5. Store at room temperature away from direct light. Do not shake or move while fermenting.
6. After 3–5 days, LAB will multiply and create a slightly sour odor. A semi-solid mat will float on top.
7. Pour off and discard the floating mat. Collect only the cloudy liquid below.

**Step 2 — Culture in Milk**
1. Add 10 parts fresh milk to 1 part fermented rice rinse water. Fill jar to 2/3 full.
2. Cover again with breathable cloth. Store at room temperature away from direct light. Do not shake.
3. After 3–5 days (longer in cooler climates), the contents will separate into:
   - A floating solid mass (similar to soft cheese — compostable, or feed to livestock)
   - A yellow liquid — **this is your LAB culture**
4. Carefully pour off the yellow liquid into a clean labeled bottle. Avoid mixing solids back in.

**Step 3 — Storage**
- Use within one week or refrigerate.
- LAB may be stored refrigerated for up to 6 months. Keep the bottle loosely capped to release fermentation gases.
- For room-temperature storage longer than one week: add an equal weight of brown sugar to the LAB culture and keep loosely capped.
- Discard if the odor becomes unpleasant (rotten). Healthy LAB smells sweet-sour.

## How to Use LAB

**Standard dilution: 1:1,000 (LAB to water)**

| Water Volume | Kitchen Measure | Fluid oz | mL |
|---|---|---|---|
| ½ gallon | ⅓ tsp | 0.06 | 2 |
| 1 gallon | ¾ tsp | 0.13 | 4 |
| 5 gallons | 1¼ Tbsp | 0.64 | 19 |
| 10 gallons | 2½ Tbsp | 1.28 | 38 |
| 25 gallons | just under ½ cup | 3.2 | 95 |

**As a foliar spray:**
- Mix 1:1,000 with water.
- Combine with FPJ for a nutritional foliar cocktail.
- Apply to leaf surfaces of leafy or fruiting crops.
- **Caution:** Over-application of LAB to fruit crops can reduce sweetness (lowered brix). Apply sparingly during the late fruiting stage.

**As a soil drench:**
- Apply alongside IMO in soil preparation to accelerate organic matter decomposition.
- Enhances release of plant nutrients.

**For seed treatment:**
- Soak seeds in LAB solution (1:1,000) along with WCA and OHN before planting.
- Improves germination rate, inoculates seeds with beneficial microbes, and helps prevent damping off (fungal seedling rot).

**For livestock:**
- Add to drinking water at 1:1,000 dilution, offered 3 times weekly.
- Supports gut flora and reduces pen odors when used with deep litter systems.

## Caution on Fruit Crops

UH-CTAHR explicitly notes: "Over-application of LAB culture to fruit crops may result in the loss of sweetness (lowered brix) of fruits." For peppers and other fruiting crops, use LAB primarily during the vegetative stage and reduce or stop during fruit development.
