## Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ)

*Category: KNF · Difficulty: Beginner*

- **Prep time:** 45 minutes active
- **Fermentation time:** 5–10 days
- **Total time:** About 7–10 days

FPJ captures vegetative growth hormones and soluble nutrition from fast-growing plant tips via brown-sugar osmosis. For peppers, it is the primary liquid ‘food’ while plants are building leaves and roots before heavy flowering.

### Ingredients

- **Vigorous shoot tips** — 1× vs sugar weight — Comfrey, sweet potato vine, or moringa—no disease
- **Unrefined brown sugar** — match plant weight
- **Breathable ferment vessel** — 1 — Bucket/jar + cloth
- **Labels + marker** — 1 *(optional)*

### Steps

1. Harvest tips near dawn when turgor is high; skip rinsing to preserve epiphytic microbes. *(Duration: 20 min · Tip: Do not use pesticide-laden weeds.)*
2. Chop to thumb-length pieces to rupture cells without liquefying the mash. *(Duration: 15 min)*
3. Alternate thin layers of plant and sugar, pressing firmly to exclude air. *(Duration: 15 min · Tip: Liquid should begin weeping within a day.)*
4. Cover with cloth; store warm-shade; date the batch.
5. When bubbling slows and aroma is tangy-sweet, strain through muslin. *(Duration: 25 min)*
6. Bottle amber liquid in glass with head space; refrigerate only if indoor temps exceed ~85°F. *(Duration: 10 min)*
7. Compost the marc or fold into an IMO3 pile to close the loop. *(Duration: 10 min)*

### Tips

- Pull back FPJ as clusters set— lingering vegetative pushes delay pod fill in some cultivars.
- Pair FPJ foliars with LAB drench on different nights to keep leaf surfaces from overloading.

### Safety notes

- Discard ferments dominated by continuous black, foul anaerobic slime or acrid ammonia with no balancing sour-sweet note.

### Application

- **Application rate:** 1:500–1:1,000 (seedlings toward the weaker end)
- **Frequency:** Every 7–10 days during vegetative growth

**Best for:** Seedlings after true leaves, Field canopy expansion, Recovery from mechanical damage

**Tags:** KNF, FPJ, foliar


## Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ)

*Category: KNF · Difficulty: Beginner*

- **Prep time:** 40 minutes
- **Fermentation time:** 7–14 days
- **Total time:** 1–2 weeks

FFJ uses ripe fruit and brown sugar to yield a reproductive-stage liquid rich in sugars, acids, and aromatics. It complements mineral inputs once peppers move from canopy building into flowering, fruit set, and ripening.

### Ingredients

- **Ripe / overripe fruit** — 1× vs sugar — Papaya, banana, fig—trim rot
- **Brown sugar** — match fruit
- **Jar + overflow tray** — 1 — Condensing greenhouses need trays

### Steps

1. Remove stems, cores, and bruises; dice fruit for dense packing. *(Duration: 15 min)*
2. Layer fruit and sugar, pressing each layer to express juice. *(Duration: 15 min)*
3. Cover with cloth; monitor overflow during warm weather. *(Tip: Ant-proof the footprint.)*
4. After activity slows, strain through muslin; discard marc to hot compost. *(Duration: 20 min)*
5. Bottle, date, and store cool-dark; smell should stay fruity-acidic. *(Duration: 10 min)*
6. Optional second soak of marc in light brine for weaker secondary press—label separately. *(Duration: 15 min)*

### Tips

- Begin FFJ as buds appear; taper FPJ so nitrogen does not swamp fruit loading.
- Mix weaker FFJ with WCA during rapid fruit expansion to buffer calcium demand.

### Safety notes

- Discard ferments dominated by continuous black, foul anaerobic slime or acrid ammonia with no balancing sour-sweet note.

### Application

- **Application rate:** 1:500–1:1,000 with clean, low-mineral water
- **Frequency:** Every 7–14 days from bloom through ripening

**Best for:** Fruit sizing, Brix finishing, Container-grown reproductive plants

**Tags:** KNF, FFJ


## Fish Amino Acid (FAA)

*Category: KNF · Difficulty: Intermediate*

- **Prep time:** 1 hour setup
- **Fermentation time:** 6–12 months
- **Total time:** Multi-month

Ferments fish scraps under brown sugar to supply amino nitrogen and trace proteins without synthetic ammonium shock. It belongs in the vegetative toolbox but always heavily diluted and rotated with microbial inputs.

### Ingredients

- **Fresh fish scraps** — 1 part — Heads & frames; keep chilled until pack
- **Brown sugar** — 1 part
- **HDPE drum with gas relief** — 1

### Steps

1. Dice fish small; alternate equal layers, pressing out air voids. *(Duration: 30 min · Tip: Work cool.)*
2. Seal with burp schedule or fermentation lock—never rigid glass without head-room. *(Duration: 20 min)*
3. Burp weekly at first; later monthly; liquid rises as solids collapse. *(Duration: 15 min)*
4. Mature FAA smells savory-fermented, not purely septic. *(Tip: If uncertain, wait longer or dilute more.)*
5. Strain; bottle amber liquid; solids bury in active compost—away from waterways. *(Duration: 40 min)*

### Tips

- Separate FAA days from heavy IMO liquid drenches so biology can process nitrogen gradually.
- Older FAA is milder—mark vintage and expect to stretch dilution on sensitive peppers.

### Safety notes

- Pressurized jars can rupture—never hermetically seal a warming batch.
- Discard ferments dominated by continuous black, foul anaerobic slime or acrid ammonia with no balancing sour-sweet note.

### Application

- **Application rate:** Begin ~1:1,200 for seedlings; vigorous adults sometimes accept 1:500
- **Frequency:** 10–14 day intervals during leafy growth phases

**Best for:** Canopy repair, Chlorosis correction when biology is already active

**Tags:** KNF, FAA, nitrogen


## Oriental Herbal Nutrient (OHN)

*Category: KNF · Difficulty: Advanced*

- **Prep time:** 2 days initial packing
- **Fermentation time:** Months of alcohol cycles
- **Total time:** Many months to years

OHN macerates five traditional botanicals through alternating alcohol and vinegar phases to produce a resilient immune-support tonic. Small amounts travel into almost every maintenance spray because it buffers stress during Florida heat snaps and wind events.

### Ingredients

- **Angelica, cinnamon, licorice, ginger, garlic (fresh/dried per CGNF guidance)** — Equal portions to start — Source food-grade, mold-free
- **Distilled or sugar-cane spirit (~30–35% abv)** — Enough to cover first soak — Local laws apply
- **High-quality brown rice vinegar (BRV)** — Reserved for later extraction cycles
- **Sterilized glass jars** — One per botanical layer

### Steps

1. Chop herbs roughly; pack each botanical separately—never moldy inputs. *(Duration: 45 min)*
2. Pour alcohol to cover; seal lightly; store dark, labeling alcohol pulls. *(Duration: 30 d first pass)*
3. Drain alcohol fraction; reserve; begin vinegar soak per schedule. *(Tip: Cycle per CGNF pacing.)*
4. Repeat alcohol/vinegar alternations until plant cellulose surrenders color. *(Tip: Patience defines quality.)*
5. Combine equal finished extracts (or prescribed ratios) into master OHN. *(Duration: 60 min · Tip: Keep notes on vintage.)*
6. Rest combined OHN; decant clear fraction; sugar-stabilize if protocol demands. *(Duration: 14 d)*
7. Bottle dark glass; OHN improves with age when stored cool. *(Duration: 30 min)*
8. Always shake before dilute use so oils redistribute. *(Duration: 2 min)*
9. Never smell-test directly from concentrate—waft vapors to avoid ethanol inhalation.
10. Document batch codes for traceability in nursery QA logs. *(Duration: 15 min)*

### Tips

- Use fractions of a teaspoon per gallon on maintenance sprays—OHN is potent.
- If licensing or alcohol sourcing blocks you, purchase inspected OHN and log supplier COA.

### Safety notes

- High-proof alcohol and vapors are flammable—no open flame.
- Potassium hydroxide and hot oils cause severe burns—nitrile gloves, eye protection, ventilation, and no aluminum vessels.

### Application

- **Application rate:** ~¾ tsp OHN per gallon when paired with FPJ + BRV maintenance cocktail
- **Frequency:** Every 10–12 days alongside normal KNF rotations

**Best for:** Heat/wind stress, Immune maintenance, Seedling hardening when extremely dilute

**Tags:** KNF, OHN, herbal


## Clean Ocean Seawater Dilution (Mineral Drench)

*Category: KNF · Difficulty: Beginner*

- **Prep time:** 20 minutes
- **Total time:** Same day

Well-sourced seawater contributes trace minerals that strengthen fruit quality and can help manage certain fungal threats when properly diluted. On Florida farms it must be captured away from harmful algal blooms or urban plume runoff.

### Ingredients

- **Clean seawater** — 1 part — Gulf/Atlantic, away from discharge
- **Non-chlorinated fresh water** — 20–30 parts — Match your ripening stage schedule
- **Salinity test refractometer** — 1 *(optional)* — Optional but wise

### Steps

1. Sample only during calm, clear tide windows; avoid red-tide advisories. *(Duration: 15 min)*
2. Filter through coffee mesh to remove debris and plankton. *(Duration: 10 min)*
3. Dilute start 1:30 for first ripening passes; tighten only with observation. *(Duration: 15 min)*
4. Apply as evening foliar or drench when stomata are open but sun won’t bake salt.
5. Log electrical conductivity if fertigating so totals stay within pepper tolerance. *(Duration: 10 min)*

### Tips

- Advance dilution strength gradually as pods mature—never jump from 1:40 to 1:10 overnight.
- Pause seawater if margins desiccate—pair with fresh OM mulches.

### Safety notes

- Salinity burns tissue when concentrated—always pre-dilute and spot-test a branch.

### Application

- **Application rate:** Commonly 1:20–1:30 during ripening (follow observation)
- **Frequency:** Every 10–14 days through final swell

**Best for:** Late brix finishing, Trace mineral supplementation

**Tags:** seawater, minerals, KNF

## Lactic Acid Bacteria Serum (LAB)

*Category: MicrobialCultures · Difficulty: Intermediate*

- **Prep time:** 30 minutes
- **Fermentation time:** 3–7 days rice wash; optional milk curd 1 day
- **Total time:** About 7–10 days

LAB serum harvests lactobacilli from rice-wash fermentation or from milk curds, then stabilizes the clarified fluid with sugar for shelf life. In humid pepper houses it keeps anaerobic pockets in check and complements IMO drenches without synthetic fungicides.

### Ingredients

- **Organic rice** — 1 cup
- **Non-chlorinated water** — 2 L
- **Whole milk (optional)** — 500 mL *(optional)* — Skip for vegan batches
- **Brown sugar** — match finished liquid volume

### Steps

1. Perform a gentle first rinse of rice; decant the cloudy fraction into a jar. *(Duration: 10 min · Tip: Later rinses are discarded.)*
2. Loosely cover; wait until the liquid sours pleasantly (~3 days). *(Tip: Temperature controls timing.)*
3. Strain rice wash liquid; optionally inoculate milk to curd and harvest whey. *(Duration: 24–48 h · Tip: Stop milk path if curds smell off.)*
4. Collect the translucent fraction beneath foam or curds. *(Duration: 15 min)*
5. Mix 1:1 with brown sugar to stabilize; stir until dissolved. *(Duration: 15 min)*
6. Bottle refrigerated; label concentration date. *(Duration: 10 min)*
7. Field-apply at extreme dilution—start at 1:2,000 on seedlings. *(Tip: Observe gloss vs. stickiness.)*

### Tips

- Chlorinated municipal water kills LAB—use filtered or rested water overnight.
- Alternate with FFJ/WCA sprays so acids do not accumulate on waxy leaves in one pass.

### Safety notes

- Do not aerosolize concentrated acid without PPE; dilute before spraying.
- Discard ferments dominated by continuous black, foul anaerobic slime or acrid ammonia with no balancing sour-sweet note.

### Application

- **Application rate:** 1:500–1:2,000
- **Frequency:** Weekly during establishment; monthly once soils stabilize

**Best for:** Humid disease pressure, Compost odor, Post-transplant establishment

**Tags:** LAB, microbes, KNF


## IMO Stage 1 — Forest Floor Rice Capture

*Category: MicrobialCultures · Difficulty: Intermediate*

- **Prep time:** Variable by stage
- **Fermentation time:** Stage-dependent
- **Total time:** Weeks to months

Capture indigenous microbes from locally adapted leaf litter using steamed rice as a substrate. It is the start of every IMO sequence and should reflect your watershed’s fungal:bacterial balance—not bagged off-site inoculants.

### Ingredients

- **Core inputs per sub-method** — See stage recipe headings — Always local
- **Non-chlorinated water** — Bulk
- **Lab notebook + temperature log** — 1

### Steps

1. Locate healthy forest edge or old-growth understory free of chemical drift. *(Duration: 30 min · Tip: Morning is ideal.)*
2. Steam rice to al dente; cool to blood temperature. *(Duration: 45 min)*
3. Place handful on bamboo tray/box; bury lightly under native litter; cover loosely. *(Duration: 20 min)*
4. Return in 3–5 days; IMO1 is ready when fungal hyphae dominate with clean white bloom. *(Duration: 5 min · Tip: Skip technicolor molds.)*
5. Harvest only what you need; leave biomass to regenerate microflora. *(Duration: 15 min · Tip: Ethical wildcrafting rules.)*
6. Document GPS + date for traceability. *(Duration: 5 min)*

### Tips

- Never equate IMO with random bagged EM—traceability to your biome matters.
- If molds discolor, consult local extension before repeating collection from the same cluster.

### Safety notes

- Wild collection respects land access rules; tick / wildlife awareness in understory work.

### Application

- **Application rate:** Liquid IMO2 commonly 1:500–1:1,000; solids site-specific
- **Frequency:** Per crop phenology—often weekly establishment then monthly

**Best for:** Soil biology foundation, Suppression via diversity, Nursery block hygiene

**Tags:** IMO, microbes, KNF

## Homemade Brown Rice Vinegar (BRV)

*Category: FermentedInputs · Difficulty: Advanced*

- **Prep time:** 1 hour
- **Fermentation time:** 6–18+ months
- **Total time:** Years for premium batches

BRV is slow dual-stage vinegar from whole brown rice—first sugar wine, then acetification. It extracts eggshell calcium for WCA/WCP and appears in nearly every KNF spray as a gentle acidifier and mildew deterrent.

### Ingredients

- **Whole brown rice** — per vessel volume — Organic if possible
- **Koji / yeast / vinegar mother** — per local starter availability *(optional)* — Mother accelerates acetate stage
- **Food-grade storage barrel or glass demijohn** — 1 — Acetobacter needs air but limits fruit fly
- **Breathable yet insect-proof vents** — 1 set

### Steps

1. Steam rice fluffy; cool; inoculate with koji or trusted wine yeast to saccharify. *(Duration: 4 h)*
2. Maintain anaerobic alcohol stage until specific gravity stabilizes. *(Duration: 14–30 d)*
3. Introduce cellulose-wrapped mother or raw vinegar back-slop; introduce oxygen gently. *(Tip: Never seal air-tight now.)*
4. Hold 60–85°F with stable oxygen; acetification may take months. *(Duration: Months)*
5. Taste monthly; stop at balanced sour—not harsh industrial acetate burn.
6. Pull finished BRV; pasteurize only if required by your SOP—otherwise raw vinegar for living extracts. *(Duration: 45 min · Tip: Pasteurization kills extract microbes.)*
7. Age in glass; acid matures; record each pull’s pH. *(Duration: Ongoing)*

### Tips

- If home BRV stalls, source verified brown rice vinegar commercially while your batch ages.
- Use the same vintage for each extraction batch so WCA saturation rates stay predictable.

### Safety notes

- Discard ferments dominated by continuous black, foul anaerobic slime or acrid ammonia with no balancing sour-sweet note.

### Application

- **Application rate:** 1:1,000 in sprays (component) or full-strength as extract solvent only
- **Frequency:** Component of maintenance sprays every 10–12 days

**Best for:** Mildew management, WCA/WCP extraction acid, Spray tank pH fine-tuning

**Tags:** BRV, vinegar, KNF


## Fermented Seawater (FSW)

*Category: FermentedInputs · Difficulty: Intermediate*

- **Prep time:** 45 minutes
- **Fermentation time:** 12–72 hours
- **Total time:** 2–4 days (weather dependent)

Fermented seawater marries diluted seawater with rice-wash microbes and a small FPJ fraction so minerals arrive with active biology. Growers on the humid Gulf and Atlantic coasts use it preventively during anthracnose pressure on ripe pods.

### Ingredients

- **Seawater** — diluted 1:30 first — Quality as per seawater recipe
- **Rice wash / LAB serum** — 1:200 in final tank — Adds lactic cohort
- **Mature FPJ** — 1:500 equivalent in tank math *(optional)* — Mugwort blends historically
- **Fresh water** — bulk of volume — Chlorine-free

### Steps

1. Build base dilution seawater per ripening plan (start weakest). *(Duration: 15 min)*
2. Add rice wash or diluted LAB before oils or acids hit the tank. *(Duration: 5 min)*
3. Add FPJ last; stir gently; avoid whipping excess foam. *(Duration: 5 min)*
4. Allow warm-weather ferments 12–24 h; extend if overcast or cool. *(Tip: Smell stays sea-mineral, not sour milk.)*
5. Strain if precipitate forms; spray dusk or dawn. *(Duration: 10 min)*
6. Clean tank jets after salt sprays to prevent clogging. *(Duration: 15 min)*

### Tips

- Alternate FSW nights with JS/JHS programs so surfaces aren’t coated daily with minerals + sulfur.
- Use greenhouse staging—first road-test low tunnels before whole block sprays.

### Safety notes

- Same salinity cautions as plain seawater; never spray drought-stressed plants at midday.

### Application

- **Application rate:** Dilution anchored to seawater recipe; biology additives stay trace
- **Frequency:** Preventive every 10–14 days during humid fruiting

**Best for:** Anthracnose-prone finishing blocks, Late-season mineral uplift

**Tags:** FSW, ferment, minerals


## Pyroligneous Acid (Wood Vinegar / WS)

*Category: FermentedInputs · Difficulty: Advanced*

- **Prep time:** 1 day kiln prep
- **Fermentation time:** Condensation capture
- **Total time:** Batch-dependent

Wood vinegar is the condensed smoke fraction from controlled biomass pyrolysis—distinct from culinary smoke flavorings. In disciplined KNF it is used in tiny dilutions for soil biology signaling—not choked into reservoir irrigation unchecked.

### Ingredients

- **Hardwood feedstock (kiln-dried chunks)** — per retort volume — Avoid treated lumber
- **Condensing train + heat-safe collection vessels** — 1 — Professional kiln recommended
- **pH / density logging notebook** — 1 *(optional)*

### Steps

1. Only proceed if local regulations and insurance cover small-batch pyrolysis. *(Duration: 60 min · Tip: Commercial source often safer.)*
2. Fraction crude pyroligneous acid into light/middle/heavy cuts during cooling. *(Tip: Discard tar-heavy heels.)*
3. Age 3–6 months vented so harsh phenolics mellow. *(Duration: Months)*
4. Dilute 1:500–1:1,000 for exploratory soil drenches; never foliar-heavy raw. *(Duration: 20 min)*

3. Let saponify until pH stabilizes per strip logging. *(Duration: 30 min)*
4. Dilute stock into labeled bottles; store cool. *(Duration: 20 min)*
5. Foam-test in a jar of your spray water before batching expensive inputs. *(Duration: 10 min)*

### Tips

- Typical spray use ~1–2 mL/L—last ingredient into the tank.
- Hard well water may need citric pretreatment—test jar foam stability.

### Safety notes

- Potassium hydroxide and hot oils cause severe burns—nitrile gloves, eye protection, ventilation, and no aluminum vessels.

### Application

- **Application rate:** ≈1–2 mL per liter of spray solution
- **Frequency:** Whenever using JS, JHS, neem, or mineral suspensions

**Best for:** Foliar coverage, Soft-bodied insect washes combined with JHS

**Tags:** JADAM, JWA, surfactant


## JADAM Sulfur (JS) Fungicide

*Category: PestControl · Difficulty: Advanced*

- **Prep time:** 1 hour
- **Total time:** Same day

JS dissolves elemental sulfur with KOH to create a powerful organic fungicide without lime-sulfur’s steel corrosion. Pepper growers lean on JS + JWA during Florida’s mildew windows but must respect heat/humidity phytotoxicity thresholds.

### Ingredients

- **Elemental sulfur (ag powder)** — 1 kg batch basis
- **Potassium hydroxide** — 1 kg (paired)
- **Rainwater** — 5–6 L — Soft water essential

### Steps

1. Combine KOH + sulfur + water gradually; expect heat—stir non-aluminum pot. *(Duration: 45 min)*
2. Dissolve fully to wine-dark solution without grit.
3. Bottle aged concentrate; label molar-ish batch strength. *(Duration: 30 min)*
4. Never mix the same day with copper sprays—phytotoxic pairings. *(Duration: 5 min)*
5. Store concentrate in opaque HDPE, log pH monthly, and shake before any dilution so suspended sulfur stays even. *(Duration: 10 min · Tip: Replace stock if crystals precipitate unevenly after cool storage.)*

### Tips

- Dilute 100:1 to 200:1 for maintenance; tighten only on confirmed infection.
- Spray early/late—never >95°F canopy temps.

### Safety notes

- Potassium hydroxide and hot oils cause severe burns—nitrile gloves, eye protection, ventilation, and no aluminum vessels.

### Application

- **Application rate:** Typically 100:1 to 500:1 dilution from concentrate (see severity tables)
- **Frequency:** At most every 7 days during active mildew—rotate modes of action

**Best for:** Powdery mildew, Certain anthracnose pressure windows

**Tags:** JS, fungicide, JADAM


## JADAM Herbal Solution (JHS)

*Category: PestControl · Difficulty: Intermediate*

- **Prep time:** 1 hour
- **Fermentation time:** 3–7 days
- **Total time:** ~1 week

JHS ferments repellant / insecticidal herbs into a stable extract used soft or hot depending on pest pressure. Farm-scaled batches respect USDA organic approved species lists—always verify before shipping peppers.

### Ingredients

- **Fresh aromatic herbs (mint, mugwort, hot peppers, etc.)** — 500 g – 1 kg — Verify OMRI/EU lists
- **Brown sugar** — match herb weight
- **Large jar with cloth seal** — 1

### Steps

1. Wash only if contaminant risk; otherwise wildcrafted herbs prefer dry-brush cleaning. *(Duration: 30 min)*
2. Chop fine; alternate herb and sugar layers. *(Duration: 30 min)*
3. Ferment until aromatic syrup stabilizes (3–7 days warm). *(Tip: Stir daily.)*
4. Strain; stabilize; store refrigerated if your bay runs >80°F nights. *(Duration: 30 min)*
5. Dilute with JWA for coverage; rotate weekly with JS to avoid resistance. *(Duration: 15 min)*

### Tips

- Spot-test each cultivar—superhots sometimes show marginal burn when oils concentrate.
- Night applications preserve pollinators on adjacent rows.

### Safety notes

- Eye/skin sensitivity to hot pepper extracts—use goggles when macerating.

### Application

- **Application rate:** Often 200:1 stock dilution window—tighten only with scouting logs
- **Frequency:** Weekly during outbreaks; back to 14 days when clean

**Best for:** Aphid complexes, Whitefly pairing with traps, Mite suppression adjunct

**Tags:** JHS, JADAM, herbal
