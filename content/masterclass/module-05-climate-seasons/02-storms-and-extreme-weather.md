# 5.2 Storms & Extreme Weather

**Outcome:** You can prep a pepper bed before a Florida blow hits, and you know the post-storm order of operations — drainage and biology first, vanity sprays never.

---

## Core

Storms are uninvited house guests. Prep the guest room before they kick the door.

The guest room is **mulch and structure**. A fungal-leaning woody mulch layer cushions impact rain, slows surface crusting, feeds the decomposers that rebuild after a flood pulse, and keeps fruit from sitting in mud splash. Bare sand in a tropical downpour is a slapstick routine: soil moves, roots expose, disease spores hitch a ride up the splash zone onto pods.

→ **[/cookbook/fungal-dominant-mulch](/cookbook/fungal-dominant-mulch)**

**Before the cell hits (hours, not vibes):**
- Confirm mulch coverage on beds you care about — especially under heavy *C. chinense* canopies like Apocalypse and Reaper.
- Clear saucers and low spots; stake or cage plants that will thrash.
- Harvest ripe or near-ripe fruit you were already planning to pick — wind + rain turns soft pods into anthracnose invitations.
- Glance at **NIMS radar / weather** so you are reacting to a real track, not a group chat rumor. The map is a heads-up, not a spell.

**After the guest leaves:**
1. Walk for standing water and broken limbs — dump water, prune wreckage, discard smashed fruit (do not compost obvious fruit-rot cases into your nicest pile).
2. Let the profile breathe. Do not "cheer up" a saturated bed with a nitrogen party.
3. When soils are workable again, run the **soil biology recovery** mindset: carbon, gentle biology, patience — the same recovery logic we use after chemical load, adapted to storm stress.

→ **[/cookbook/soil-biology-recovery-program](/cookbook/soil-biology-recovery-program)**

Wet-season fruit rot (anthracnose pressure) is a Florida tax. Cultural humidity control plus the CookBook preventive spray path beat heroics after lesions crater the pod.

→ **[/cookbook/anthracnose-suppression-spray](/cookbook/anthracnose-suppression-spray)**

You are not trying to stop the sky. You are trying to make sure the sky does not erase six months of soil work in one afternoon.

## 🌶️ Deeper Heat

### What storms actually do to a pepper system

- **Physical:** lodging, abrasion, mulch wash-off, container blow-over.
- **Hydrologic:** saturation → oxygen crash → root damage → secondary wilt.
- **Epidemiologic:** splash dispersal of *Colletotrichum* and friends onto fruit; prolonged leaf wetness windows.

Anthracnose shows as sunken, dark fruit lesions — classic wet-season heartbreak on chinense pods. Prevention is cultural (airflow, mulch splash barrier, timely harvest) plus the CookBook spray rotations when pressure justifies it. [VERIFY vs CookBook: anthracnose-suppression-spray]

### Mulch as storm infrastructure

Fungal-dominant woody mulch is not décor. Partially composted chips + IMO4 dusting (per CookBook) feed fungi that partner with pepper roots and keep a sacrificial layer between raindrop and soil. [VERIFY vs CookBook: fungal-dominant-mulch]

If a storm strips mulch bare, **replace the armor** before the next cell. Leaving bare soil "to dry faster" often means crust + splash + biology starvation.

### Recovery sequencing (do not scramble)

| Phase | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| 0–24 h | Drain, upright, remove trash fruit, photo-log damage in NIMS | Flood with fertilizer "to help them bounce back" |
| Soil workable | Re-mulch gaps; consider biology recovery program steps | Till saturated beds into pudding |
| Pressure rising | Anthracnose preventive path per CookBook + scouting | Calendar-spray every bottle you own |

Soil biology recovery after chemical load is the cousin of post-storm recovery: rebuild carbon and living communities deliberately. Read the CookBook page for phase logic — do not invent a bleach-and-pray protocol. [VERIFY vs CookBook: soil-biology-recovery-program]

### Heat + wind without rain

Extreme weather is not only hurricanes. Dry wind and 95°F+ afternoons desiccate containers and scorch west-facing leaves. Mulch and afternoon shade cloth can matter as much as storm stakes. Same literacy: observe, then act.

### What this is *not*

This lesson is not a promise that biological sprays make plants storm-proof. They reduce disease pressure and help recovery. Structural prep and drainage still do the heavy lifting.
