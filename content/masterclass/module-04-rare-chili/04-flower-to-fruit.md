# 4.4 Flower to Fruit

**Outcome:** You can move a chili from bloom to set with calcium discipline, reproductive-stage FFJ, and a blossom-end-rot protocol that fixes delivery — not just the spray bottle.

---

## Core

Calcium is mortar in the expanding brick walls of fruit cells.

When a pepper fruit swells, new cell walls go up fast. **Calcium** is a structural mineral that helps those walls hold — but it moves mostly with the water stream through the plant (**xylem** transport), not like a mobile rescue squad you can summon from old leaves on demand. Uneven irrigation, wild transpiration swings, or a canopy that never delivers flow to the far end of a fruit creates **blossom-end rot (BER)** — the classic leathery black patch on the blossom end — even when a soil test says "enough calcium."

IC SPICY's flower-to-fruit kit:

- **WCA** — water-soluble calcium from eggshell + vinegar chemistry; foliar-available when diluted correctly.
- **Bud-break spray (WCA + FFJ)** — reproductive minerals + fruit-stage sugars as clusters form.
- **FFJ** — fermented ripe fruit juice; reproductive-stage tonic (not FPJ's vegetative job).
- **BER prevention protocol** — irrigation uniformity + transpiration management + WCA timing as one system.

Reaper-class fruit and other catalog heavy-hitters (open varieties 840 and 1 on Pepperpedia for their names and fruit habits) all obey the same physics: mortar on schedule beats panic calcium after the scar appears.

**One practical takeaway for tonight:** if BER shows up, fix watering rhythm and canopy flow first — then follow the CookBook prevention card — before you drown the plant in concentrate.

## 🌶️ Deeper Heat

### WCA number

From the CookBook WCA card:

- Foliar typical dilution **1:1,000**. [VERIFY vs CookBook: water-soluble-calcium-wca]
- Often every **7–10 days** during rapid pod expansion. [VERIFY vs CookBook: water-soluble-calcium-wca]

Apply after peak transpiration (dusk tip on the card) so young fruits can take up what they need. Rotate nights with seawater sprays later so salinity and calcium stay balanced (CookBook tip; ripening detail in 4.5).

WCA/WCP mineral tools also appear in Module 2.4's calcium lesson — same kitchen, fruiting stage focus here.

### FFJ numbers

From the CookBook FFJ card:

- Dilution **1:500–1:1,000**. [VERIFY vs CookBook: fermented-fruit-juice-ffj]
- Every **7–14 days** from bloom through ripening. [VERIFY vs CookBook: fermented-fruit-juice-ffj]

Begin FFJ as buds appear; taper FPJ so nitrogen does not swamp fruit loading (CookBook tip). Weaker FFJ mixed with WCA during rapid expansion is a documented pairing on the FFJ card.

### Bud-break and BER protocol

- **Bud-break (WCA + FFJ):** [/cookbook/bud-break-wca-ffj-spray](/cookbook/bud-break-wca-ffj-spray) — threshold/SOP-based; not a substitute for irrigation EC control.
- **BER prevention:** [/cookbook/blossom-end-rot-prevention](/cookbook/blossom-end-rot-prevention) — integrates WCA timing, irrigation uniformity, and transpiration management so calcium reaches distal fruits.

### Pollination awareness

Peppers are largely self-fertile, but humidity extremes, heat stress, and zero airflow can still drop set. Gentle shake, pollinator habitat, and avoiding midday stress sprays help more than mythical "bloom booster" overdoses.

### Failure modes

- Spraying undiluted WCA → leaf burn and wasted mortar.
- Keeping FPJ weekly into heavy fruiting → leafy monster, lazy pods.
- Treating BER as a one-spray disease → ignoring the water/flow problem.
