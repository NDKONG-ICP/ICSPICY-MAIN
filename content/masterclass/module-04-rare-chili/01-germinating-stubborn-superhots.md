# 4.1 Germinating Stubborn Superhots

**Outcome:** You can wake Reaper-class seed without folklore chaos — heat and humidity first, CookBook priming when you choose biology assists, and a transplant drench that settles roots instead of shocking them.

---

## Core

Germinating a stubborn superhot is waking a stubborn dragon egg.

Carolina Reaper, Ghost Pepper, and Apocalypse Scorpion seeds did not evolve to sprout on a cold windowsill because you are impatient. Thick seed coats, variable dormancy, and *C. chinense* temperament mean the egg opens when **warmth, moisture, and oxygen** stay in the right band long enough — not when you poke it every hour.

IC SPICY order of operations:

1. **Read the variety guide** for that pepper's germination personality (Pepperpedia links below).
2. **Dial environment** — consistent warmth, evenly moist (not swampy) media, gentle air, light after emergence.
3. **Optional biology assist** — the CookBook seed-priming soak (WCA + OHN + LAB) when you want a controlled pre-sow soak. Ratios and soak windows are **threshold-based on the recipe card** — do not invent a kitchen-sink dilution from memory.
4. **After transplant** — an IMO + LAB establishment drench within the CookBook window to reduce shock and jumpstart the rhizosheath (the living sleeve of microbes around new roots).

**One practical takeaway for tonight:** if a tray fails, log temperature and moisture before you blame the vendor. Dragons hatch on climate; potions are optional.

---

## 🌶️ Deeper Heat

### Why superhots stall

Many *C. chinense* seeds carry thicker coats and slower metabolic wake-ups than a Jalapeño. Cool nights, soggy media, and wild temperature swings create rot before radicles. Sterile media helps sanitation; lifeless media still needs careful watering because there is no soil buffer yet.

### Priming soak — point to the card

The CookBook **Seed Priming Soak (WCA + OHN + LAB)** combines dilute mineral calcium, herbal immune tone (OHN), and lactic biology (LAB) for a controlled soak before sowing. Steps on the card include assembling mature inputs to a soak EC target, soaking on a time window that respects pepper tolerance, draining, brief air-dry, then sowing immediately with an emergence log vs untreated controls.

**Do not invent soak ratios in this lesson.** Open the recipe and follow it:

→ [/cookbook/seed-priming-soak-wca-ohn-lab](/cookbook/seed-priming-soak-wca-ohn-lab)

LAB elsewhere in the CookBook commonly dilutes **1:500–1:2,000** for field/serum uses — that is *not* automatically your seed-soak math. [VERIFY vs CookBook: lactic-acid-bacteria-lab] Seed soak targets live on the priming card.

### Transplant drench

Once seedlings move up, the **Transplant Establishment Drench (IMO liquids + LAB)** is scheduled in the CookBook as an establishment tool (within the recipe's timing logic — follow the card, not a calendar superstition). It links dilute IMO2 with LAB to reduce transplant shock. Application is threshold/SOP-based on the recipe page:

→ [/cookbook/transplant-imo-lab-drench](/cookbook/transplant-imo-lab-drench)

Liquid IMO2 in the IMO set is commonly **1:500–1:1,000** when used as a liquid inoculant. [VERIFY vs CookBook: imo-stage-2-liquid-culture]
