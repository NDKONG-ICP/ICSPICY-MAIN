# 6.1 Harvest & Handling

**Outcome:** You can pick, move, and stage chili pods so quality survives the trip from plant to board — with basic food-safety habits from public guidance, not kitchen mythology.

---

## Core

Harvest handling is the pit crew after the race. Do not drop the trophy.

You spent months on soil, water, and weather literacy. The pod on the plant is the finish line photo. Everything after — clipping, carrying, rinsing, staging — either protects that work or wastes it. Soft bruises become rot. Dirty boards become cross-contamination. Bare-hand Reaper oil becomes a face-touching regret you will remember.

**Pick with intent.**
- Harvest when the variety's ripe color is true (Pepperpedia guides beat neighbor folklore).
- Use clean shears or a gentle twist that does not strip bark from next week's wood.
- Carry in shallow trays, not a deep sack that crushes the bottom layer — Scotch Bonnet and other thick-wall chinense bruise when stacked like gravel.
- Keep hot pods away from your eyes, contacts, and pets. Gloves are not cosplay; they are strategy.

**Stage like a crew chief.**
- Sort out damaged, bird-pecked, or anthracnose-suspect fruit — those go to trash or a clearly separated "cook hot now" pile, not into a long ferment.
- Cool, ventilated holding beats a sealed plastic bag of warm peppers sweating into a science experiment.
- Wash produce under running water when you are ready to cut; do not use soap or detergent on food. Public food-safety guidance is boring on purpose — follow it.

**Heat etiquette.** Reaper and Scotch Bonnet oils travel. Dedicated cutting board or a hard scrub between tasks. Do not touch your face. If skin burns, wash with soap and water; oil-based cleaners help some people more than water alone — still not medical advice, just grower folklore that sometimes works. For real reactions, use public health guidance and common sense.

This lesson has **no primary CookBook slug** — handling lives in kitchen discipline and Pepperpedia timing. Module 6.3 covers fermentation safety; Module 6.4 covers proving where the pod came from.

## 🌶️ Deeper Heat

### Quality clocks (why speed matters)

After harvest, respiration and moisture loss continue. Warm piles sweat. Soft fruit invites molds. For sauce or ferment destined for jars, starting with sound fruit is the cheapest "preservative" you will ever use.

### Sort grades (practical, not USDA theater)

| Grade | Use |
|---|---|
| Sound, fully ripe | Fresh, dehydrator, showcase sauce, ferment |
| Minor cosmetic scars | Cook hot soon; avoid long ambient holds |
| Soft, mold, sunken lesions | Discard — do not "save" into a crock |

Anthracnose lesions from wet season (Lesson 5.2) are not a flavor accent. Remove them from the craft line.

### Food safety — stick to public rails

IC SPICY masterclass is grower education, not a processing plant HACCP manual. For home kitchens:
- Clean hands, clean tools, clean surfaces.
- Running water rinse for produce; no soap on peppers.
- Separate raw meat boards from produce boards.
- Refrigerate cut produce promptly; follow USDA / FDA home food-safety basics for leftovers and acidified foods (detail in 6.3).

We do **not** invent sanitizer recipes or medical claims here. Link public sources in Further Reading.

### Variety notes (handling personality)

- **Red Habanero (10)** — frequent picks; good practice cadence for gentle handling.
- **Scotch Bonnet (1)** — heavy fruit; tray discipline matters.
- **Reaper (7)** — oil discipline matters as much as bruise discipline.

### What this is *not*

This is not a scavenger-hunt for every pepper disease. If fruit is questionable, out it goes. Pride is not an ingredient.
