# 2.2 OHN — Oriental Herbal Nutrient

**Outcome:** You can tell a neighbor what OHN is for, why the dose is tiny, and point them at the IC SPICY CookBook recipe — without turning your spray shelf into a mystery shrine.

---

## Core

OHN is the house hot sauce of the spray program.

You do not pour it on like broth. You do not drink it. You add a little — and the whole plate wakes up. Oriental Herbal Nutrient is a long, patient extract of traditional botanicals (think garlic, ginger, and friends in equal starting portions) pulled through alcohol and vinegar phases until you have a dark, potent tonic. On peppers it rides along as **vitality and stress insurance**, not as the main meal. FPJ feeds the growth spurt. OHN is the attitude in the tank — especially useful when a Reaper or Apocalypse Scorpion is taking heat, wind, or transplant sulk and you want resilience without turning the program into a daily herbal bath.

That attitude is expensive in time. Real OHN takes months of cycles, not a weekend TikTok ferment. Spirit strength lands around **~30–35% abv** for the alcohol pulls; botanicals start in **equal portions**; moldy herbs are a hard no. [VERIFY vs CookBook: oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn] If sourcing alcohol or licensing blocks you, buying inspected OHN and logging the supplier is a valid nursery move — ego is not an input.

**How little is little?** Maintenance use is on the order of **~¾ tsp OHN per gallon** when paired with FPJ + BRV, about every **10–12 days**. [VERIFY vs CookBook: oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn] That is hot-sauce logic: more is not more. More is leaf drama and wasted gold.

IC SPICY's written method lives here:

→ **[/cookbook/oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn](/cookbook/oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn)**

Read that page for packing, cycle timing, and safety (flammable vapors, no open flame). This lesson is the *why and how little* so you do not treat OHN like a weekly fertilizer. Once it is on the shelf, it usually rides inside the maintenance cocktail with FPJ and BRV — still as the spicy fraction, not the base broth.

**One practical takeaway for tonight:** if your "maintenance spray" smells like a herb shop exploded in the tank, you overshot. Dial back to micro-doses and let FPJ / BRV do the volume work.

## 🌶️ Deeper Heat

### What OHN is doing (and not doing)

OHN is framed in KNF practice as an immune-support / resilience tonic — herbal extracts that help plants handle heat, wind, and general stress when diluted into a program. It is **not** a standalone pesticide, not a nitrogen source, and not a cure for bad irrigation. Think preventative seasoning, not emergency surgery.

The botanicals and alcohol/vinegar cycling are the method; the chemistry is complex and recipe-specific. Do not invent hormone names for the jar. Follow the CookBook; credit the lineage in Further Reading; keep nursery SOPs boring and repeatable.

### Maintenance cocktail — the Tuesday spray

Once FPJ, OHN, and BRV are on the shelf, the backbone mix is the maintenance foliar cocktail:

- Blend **equal micro-doses** of FPJ, OHN, and BRV at about **~1:500 each** into the tank. [VERIFY vs CookBook: maintenance-foliar-cocktail]
- Spot-test flagship plants before house-wide sprays.
- Dawn or dusk, calm air; stop if margins crinkle overnight.
- Soil drench volumes, when used that way, often land around **10–20 gal finished mix / 1000 sq ft**. [VERIFY vs CookBook: maintenance-foliar-cocktail]

Full page: [/cookbook/maintenance-foliar-cocktail](/cookbook/maintenance-foliar-cocktail)

OHN's ~¾ tsp/gallon note and the cocktail's ~1:500 micro-dose language are the same philosophy in two units — **tiny herbal fraction, disciplined dilution.** Cross-check the CookBook tables for your tank size before you argue with a measuring spoon.

### Seed priming (advanced lane)

A separate CookBook tool soaks seed with a WCA + OHN + LAB priming mix for hardening and early resilience:

→ [/cookbook/seed-priming-soak-wca-ohn-lab](/cookbook/seed-priming-soak-wca-ohn-lab)

That is not a foliar recipe with a new name. Do not cross-contaminate jar lids. Seed work is measured, brief, and logged — especially on expensive superhot lots (Reaper, Apocalypse Scorpion).

### Failure modes

- Treating OHN like FPJ volume → phytotoxicity and empty bottle.
- Sealed chaos with high-proof alcohol → fire/vapor hazard; follow safety notes.
- Moldy or mystery botanicals → trash the batch; do not "save" it with more sugar.
- Daily OHN firehose on stressed, droughted plants → you seasoned a patient in the ER.
