# 3.4 Mulch & Cover Systems

**Outcome:** You can design a surface cover that shelters microbes, buffers Florida sun and storm swings, and supports both annual practice plants and perennial superhots through winter TLC.

---

## Core

Mulch is a hotel roof for microbes in Florida sun.

Leave soil bare in Port Charlotte July and you are not "letting it breathe." You are running a rooftop barbecue on the workforce you hired in Lessons 3.1–3.3. **Mulch** — a surface layer of organic cover — shades the soil, slows evaporation, softens raindrop impact, and feeds fungi as it breaks down. The organisms under that roof keep cycling nutrients while your Jalapeño forgives a mistake and your Apocalypse Scorpion tells on you if the bed bakes.

IC SPICY leans **fungal-dominant mulch** for chili beds that want longer-term structure: woodier carbons, leaf litter, and materials that favor hyphal networks over a pure grass-clipping slime layer. **Leaf-mold understory** is the quiet luxury suite — decomposed leaves that hold moisture and biology like a forest floor lite. In Zone 10a, peppers can behave like near-perennials; winter TLC is less about burying plants in snow and more about wind, odd cold snaps, and keeping the root zone covered while tops rest or keep ticking.

**One practical takeaway for tonight:** if you can see more bare sand than cover between plants, the hotel is closed — open the CookBook mulch and winter-TLC cards and put a roof back on before the next heat spike.

## 🌶️ Deeper Heat

### What mulch is doing (mechanisms, not mysticism)

- **Microclimate:** lower surface temperature, higher humidity at the soil skin, less crack-and-seal crusting after storms.
- **Erosion & splash:** rain hits mulch first; soil stays put; disease splash from soil to lower leaves drops.
- **Carbon drip-feed:** as fungi and bacteria digest the layer, they build aggregates and hold nutrients in biomass until grazers release them (Lesson 1.1 loop).
- **Living vs dead cover:** chop-and-drop and living mulches (where appropriate) keep roots in the game; dead mulch is still a roof when living cover is not ready.

Fungal-leaning materials (wood chips aged appropriately, leaf mold, shredded woody debris) support the networks no-till beds need. Fresh high-nitrogen greens alone can mat, go anaerobic, and smell like a mistake — balance the menu.

### Zone 10a perennial logic

South Florida's cool/dry season is prime fruiting weather; the hot/wet season is survival school. Perennial or overwintered *C. chinense* plants need continuous root-zone cover more than a northern "put the garden to bed" ritual. The CookBook winter TLC card is the nursery SOP for that reality — follow it rather than importing Midwest mulch folklore wholesale.

### Practice plants vs divas

| Plant | Why it is in this lesson |
|---|---|
| Jalapeño (28) | Forgiving while you learn mulch depth and irrigation under cover |
| Apocalypse Scorpion (41) | Punishes bare, swinging moisture — good feedback plant |

### Failure modes

- Mulch piled against stems → collar rot risk; keep a small collar gap.
- Plastic-only thinking → useful in niches, but biology still wants organic food eventually.
- "Set and forget" forever → replenish as layers thin; Florida sun eats carbon.
