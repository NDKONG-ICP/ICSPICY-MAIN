# 3.2 IMO Collection & Culturing

**Outcome:** You can walk Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO) from forest-floor capture to a finished bed amendment — and you know which CookBook stage page owns each number so you do not invent ratios in the field.

---

## Core

IMO is recruiting a local band instead of importing a stadium act.

**Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO)** are microbes collected from *your* watershed — leaf litter, forest edge, healthy understory — then cultured through staged recipes until you have a soil amendment that already speaks Florida humidity, sand, and summer storms. Bagged "EM" from a catalog can be useful in a pinch. It is not the same as a crew that evolved next to your nursery.

The ladder is simple if you refuse to skip rungs:

1. **IMO1** — steamed rice under litter; wait for clean white fungal bloom.
2. **IMO2** — sugar-stabilize that rice into a liquid stock.
3. **IMO3** — expand the stock into a cool field compost pile.
4. **IMO4** — blend matured IMO3 with local soil/compost, cure, then topdress.
5. **IMO5** (optional) — fish-enhanced expansion for specific fungal pushes; not a beginner default on annual peppers.

IC SPICY's written methods live in the CookBook stage pages linked below — not in a scraped course PDF. This lesson is the map so those recipes make sense when your bamboo box smells like a forest floor and your neighbor asks why you are burying rice.

**One practical takeaway for tonight:** do not buy a mystery powder labeled "soil probiotics" and call it IMO. Collect once, culture carefully, and let the CookBook timings tell you when each stage is done.

## 🌶️ Deeper Heat

### Stage cues and CookBook numbers

| Stage | What “done” looks like | Key number (follow recipe) |
|---|---|---|
| IMO1 | Clean white hyphae dominate; skip technicolor molds | Return in **3–5 days** [VERIFY vs CookBook: imo-stage-1-rice-capture] |
| IMO2 | Dense sweet-sour syrup stock | IMO1 rice **1:1** by weight with brown sugar; collect after **7 days** [VERIFY vs CookBook: imo-stage-2-liquid-culture] |
| IMO3 | Friable, sweet-earth compost | Dilute IMO2 liquid **1:500–1:1,000**; turn when core hits **~50°C (122°F) max** [VERIFY vs CookBook: imo-stage-3-field-compost] |
| IMO4 | Uniform inoculated amendment | Moisture **~40%**; cure **7–14 days** under shade, turn twice weekly [VERIFY vs CookBook: imo-stage-4-soil-amendment] |
| IMO5 | Optional fish-steered expansion | Master IMO4 first; use cautiously on peppers [VERIFY vs CookBook: imo-stage-5-fish-enhanced-imo] |

Liquid IMO2 application rate in the CookBook set is commonly **1:500–1:1,000** (solids stay site-specific). [VERIFY vs CookBook: imo-stage-2-liquid-culture]

### Why local beats imported

Microbial communities are habitat-specific. A forest-edge capture near your farm carries organisms already tolerant of your rainfall pattern and litter chemistry. That is the point of "indigenous." You are not chasing a brand — you are cloning a neighborhood that works.

### Failure modes (so you invent fewer)

- Colored molds on IMO1 → discard that capture; try a cleaner litter site.
- Sealed airtight IMO2 → wrong ecology; breathable cover, packed to exclude *excess* air per recipe — follow the stage page.
- IMO3 cooked past guidance → you pasteurized your band; turn earlier next time at the ~50°C cue.
- Spreading unfinished IMO4 → anaerobic pockets and wasted work; finish the cure.
